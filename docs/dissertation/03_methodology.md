# 3. Methodology

## 3.1 Introduction and research design

**Design.** The study is quantitative and empirical, conducted on secondary data: a single
lender's historical export of funded green loans. No new data were collected, no human
participants were involved, and the analysis is observational — the lender's past underwriting
decisions, not the researcher's, determined which loans appear in the book. That design choice
is not incidental to the findings; it is the reason the funded-book selection effect (§5.5) and
the evaluation-versus-deployment population mismatch (§6.4) are treated as first-order
limitations rather than footnotes.

Within that frame the work is structured as a **controlled comparison under a pre-specified
protocol**. The metric panel, the label scheme, the cross-validation design and the feasibility
gates were fixed before modelling, so that the answer to each research question would be
interpretable whichever way it came out. Two features of the design deserve emphasis because
they shape every result that follows. First, the comparison is *paired*: all model and imbalance
treatments are evaluated on identical cross-validation folds, so differences between them are
not confounded by different data splits. Second, uncertainty is reported for every claim — fold
percentile bands for cross-validated metrics, bootstrap intervals for calibration and decision
metrics — because at 50 events a point estimate on its own is not evidence.

**Approach to reproducibility.** Every number in this chapter and the next is generated by a
seeded, versioned script in the project repository and can be reproduced with a single command
per stage (Appendix D); the global seed is 20260609. Figures are regenerated, never hand-edited.

**Chapter structure.** The chapter covers the dataset and label construction, the leakage audit
and data cleaning, the experimental protocol, and the calibration, explainability,
fairness-gating, decision-layer and serving components evaluated in Chapter 4.

## 3.2 The dataset

The raw data comprise **14,135 funded green loans × 166 columns**, exported as
`All_Funded_2019_Green Loan.xlsx`. Despite the filename, origination dates (`Start`) span
2015–2020, with 13,119 loans (92.8%) originated in 2019. The outcome column `Deal Status` takes
four values:

| `Deal Status` | n |
|---|---|
| current | 10,124 |
| paidOff | 3,848 |
| default | 49 |
| behind | 1 |
| (missing) | 113 |

Delinquency — the event of interest — is defined as `default` ∪ `behind`: **50 events**. This
count, not the imbalance ratio, is the binding constraint of the entire study, and no analytical
choice downstream can increase it.

## 3.3 Label construction and censoring

Loans recorded as `current` are right-censored: they had not resolved by the data snapshot, and
the censoring is mechanically related to cohort age — 72.8% of the 2019 cohort is `current`,
versus 54.5% of 2017 and 11.1% of 2016. Treating censored loans as non-events would therefore
contaminate the label with exposure time. Two label schemes were evaluated:

| Scheme | n | events | prevalence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `all_favourable` (current + paidOff = non-event) | 14,022 | 50 | 0.357% |
| `paidoff_only` (censored rows dropped) | 3,898 | 50 | 1.283% |

**`paidoff_only` is the primary label**: it restricts the non-event class to loans with a
terminal outcome, removing the censoring contamination at the cost of sample size. The event
count is identical under both schemes — dropping censored rows repairs the *ratio*, never the
*count* — which is the first demonstration of the dissertation's central theme. The scheme's own
cost is acknowledged at the outset: conditioning non-events on resolution means the evaluation
population (terminal-outcome loans) is not the deployment population (all incoming
applications), a mismatch analysed in §6.4. The single
`behind` case is retained in the event definition but is analytically irrelevant at n = 1.
Chapter 4 reports a survival-analysis feasibility check that asks whether the 10,124 censored
loans could instead be recovered through time-to-event modelling.

## 3.4 Leakage audit: default-deny feature vetting

Post-funding information is abundant in the export (payment behaviour, collection notes, closure
fields) and would trivially predict the label. The audit therefore operates **default-deny**:
every one of the 166 columns is forbidden unless explicitly vetted as knowable at application
time. **17 pre-funding features were admitted** (10 numeric, 7 categorical; among them
`Credit Score`, `Revenue`, `Average Monthly Sales`, `Time In Business`, `Amount Sought`,
`Industry`, `Borrower State`, `Channel`); the remaining ~148 columns are excluded, including all
fully-missing columns (11 columns at 100% missingness) and those above a 40% missingness
threshold. The permitted list is frozen in a versioned catalogue
(`data/governance/feature_catalogue.yaml`), and an automated assertion (`assert_no_leakage`)
fails the pipeline if any forbidden column becomes reachable — the guard is exercised in the test
suite rather than trusted on inspection.

## 3.5 Data-quality cleaning

A rule-based, leakage-safe cleaning pass corrects **54 impossible values**, each rule justified
by domain logic rather than distributional information: `Credit Score = 0` (2 rows;
missing-coded, as FICO scores cannot be zero), negative `Time In Business` (1 row), and
`Time In Business` > 600 months (51 rows; more than 50 years in business is implausible for this
book and consistent with a year/month entry error). Corrections set values to missing rather
than imputing them, leaving imputation to the in-fold pipeline. Chapter 4 reports a sensitivity
analysis confirming that no headline finding depends on this cleaning.

## 3.6 Experimental protocol

**Cross-validation.** 5-fold stratified cross-validation repeated 5 times (25 folds), so each
test fold contains approximately ten events. Results are reported as the **median and the
[2.5th, 97.5th] percentile band across folds** — never a single number. Stratification preserves
the event ratio per fold; repetition averages split-to-split variance, which dominates at this n
(Arlot and Celisse, 2010; Krstajić et al., 2014).

**In-fold discipline.** All data-dependent steps — median imputation with missing-indicators,
categorical encoding, scaling (for the linear model), and SMOTE where used — are fitted on the
training fold only, inside a single scikit-learn `Pipeline`/`ColumnTransformer` (Santos et al.,
2018; Kapoor and Narayanan, 2023). After encoding, the 17 permitted features become 25 model
inputs.

**Models × imbalance treatments.** Regularised logistic regression (the EPV-appropriate
baseline) and XGBoost (the gradient-boosted challenger), each under two imbalance treatments:
class weighting (`class_weight='balanced'` / `scale_pos_weight`) and SMOTE-within-fold — four
combinations plus a prevalence-rate dummy floor. No inner-loop hyperparameter search is
performed: with ~10 positives per fold, model selection on the inner split would itself be noise
(regularisation strength is fixed at C = 1.0).

**Metric panel (pre-specified).** (i) **PR-AUC** — primary discrimination metric; the
prevalence floor is ≈ 0.0128 under the primary label. (ii) **Recall at the top decile** — the
operational quantity: the share of all defaults captured if the desk reviews the riskiest 10%.
(iii) **Within-minority ECE** — computed on the events only. Because every event's true label
is 1, this statistic reduces to the (binned) mean confidence shortfall on actual defaults; it is
therefore an *event-level confidence* measure — the quantity an adverse-action review depends on
— rather than a bin-frequency calibration curve, and it is named and interpreted as such
wherever it appears. It carries high variance at 50 events and is always reported with its
interval. **Accuracy is excluded by design** (a constant predictor achieves 99.64% on the raw
book). Bootstrap 95%
confidence intervals accompany calibration metrics (2,000 resamples); fold-percentile bands
accompany CV metrics.

**Evidence-of-learning checks.** Because a small-n result can be an artefact, four checks attack
the headline numbers: a **label-permutation test** (100 shuffled-label reruns of the full
out-of-fold pipeline), **seed stability** (five CV seeds), a **learning curve** (50/75/100% of
data), and **single-feature baselines** against the full model.

## 3.7 Calibration and conformal prediction (RQ2)

Platt scaling and isotonic regression are fitted on a held-out calibration slice *inside* each
fold and evaluated out-of-fold, with 2,000-sample bootstrap CIs on marginal Brier score and
within-minority ECE. Split conformal prediction (LAC score) is implemented over 30 repeated
60/20/20 train/calibration/test splits at nominal coverage 0.90 and 0.95, reported — per the
protocol — as a transparency artefact with its mean set size, since marginal coverage
carries almost no information at this prevalence.

## 3.8 Explainability (RQ3)

Explanations use exact linear SHAP (Lundberg and Lee, 2017): for a linear model the attribution
is `coefficient × (x − E[x])` per feature, computed in closed form — no sampling approximation
and therefore no fidelity gap. Post-encoding attributions are aggregated back to the 17 named source
features so that explanations are legible to a non-technical reviewer. Global importance
(mean |SHAP|) is checked for coherence against the model's coefficients; local explanations
provide the signed top-3 "reason codes" per applicant used in the decision-support application.

## 3.9 Fairness feasibility protocol (RQ3)

Group-conditional fairness metrics (equalised odds, predictive parity) require events *within
each group cell*. Before any fairness metric is computed, each candidate grouping (`Industry`,
27 groups; `Borrower State`, 51 groups) is tested against a minimum of **10 events per cell** for
a metric to be reported as estimable. Cells failing the gate are reported as non-estimable rather
than silently averaged — a protocol decision made at Phase 1, before modelling.

## 3.10 Decision layer

The scored probabilities feed an expected-cost review policy: the threshold *t* minimises
`cost(t) = R·FN(t) + FP(t)` on out-of-fold scores, where R is the desk's cost ratio (one missed
default to one needless review), following Elkan (2001). Because R is not known for this desk,
the policy is reported as a sensitivity curve across R ∈ {5, 10, 20, 50, 100}, and the robustness
of the saving versus a naive 0.5 cut-off is assessed by bootstrapping the out-of-fold rows (500
resamples). The threshold is chosen empirically rather than analytically because the underlying
probabilities are miscalibrated (Chapter 4, RQ2).

## 3.11 Proof-of-concept decision-support application

The frozen class-weighted logistic regression is served through a FastAPI application
(`python -m emerald_ai serve`). The primary interface is batch scoring: a CSV or Excel upload
(including the raw dataset as-is) is ranked by risk, with the riskiest within-batch decile
flagged as the review queue; scoring is vectorised over the whole file. A secondary single-application
panel serves explanation and what-if analysis, returning the predicted score, the
operating-point flag (threshold P ≥ 0.617, the out-of-fold top-decile cut, 62% default
catch-rate; see `reports/decision_policy.md`), and signed top-3 SHAP reason codes in plain
English. This catch-rate operating point is used because the desk's cost ratio R is unknown;
§4.6 shows how it generalises to cost-optimal thresholds once R is supplied. The
application is hardened to a defensible proof-of-concept standard — structured request logging,
input validation with clean 4xx/5xx JSON errors, and optional API-key access control — and its
user interface states explicitly that the model ranks applications for review and does not
approve or decline. The engineering and its limits are analysed in Chapter 6.

## 3.12 Summary

Each stage is a CLI verb over the same audited pipeline (`eda`, `bakeoff`, `evidence`,
`calibrate`, `explain`, `improve`, `survival-check`, `decide`, `sensitivity`, `serve`); all
randomness flows from the single global seed; generated reports and figures are committed
alongside the code; and the test suite (49 tests) covers the leakage guard, the metric panel,
the decision policy, and the serving contract including its error paths. The repository is the
dissertation's evidentiary record: any figure that cannot be regenerated from it does not appear
in this document.
